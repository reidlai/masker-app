# Accessibility Review — masker-app UX Spine Pair

## Overall verdict

The new BLE permission-priming content is well-intentioned and better than the industry default ("frame before you ask"), but it is not built out far enough to be safe for this population. It models permission grant as a clean binary (granted/denied) when the real Android/iOS permission surface is not binary, it leaves the single most safety-critical signal in the whole app — "are you actually being monitored right now" — with no accessible equivalent when the notification isn't in view, and it hand-waves the exact moment (native dialog wording) where an anxious, low-tech, half-asleep patient is most likely to get scared and bail. Two of the "Confirm at build" assumptions sit directly on top of accessibility-critical and safety-critical decisions, which is the wrong place to defer.

## Findings

- **critical** State_BlePermissionPriming / State_BlePermissionDenied model permission as a clean binary, but Android's `BLUETOOTH_SCAN` and `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` are two separate runtime permissions requested together — the OS shows them as sequential dialogs, not the single dialog the spec assumes. (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Interaction Primitives "OS Permission Hand-off": *"triggers exactly one native system permission dialog"*; §State Patterns #10–11). *Why it matters:* A user can grant one and deny the other (e.g. grant SCAN, deny CONNECT). Nothing in `State_BlePermissionDenied` or `BlePermissionPrimerOrganism` describes this partial-grant state — the app would either crash the assumption that "granted" is atomic, silently limp along with a scan-but-can't-connect UX, or need engineers to invent an unreviewed third state for a safety-critical flow at build time. *Fix:* Add an explicit `State_BlePermissionPartial` (or equivalent) to the spine and specify its copy/CTA now, not at build.
- **critical** No accessible in-app equivalent exists for "monitoring is active" while the app is foregrounded during `State_MonitoringActive`. (`DESIGN.md` Night Mode Invariant; `EXPERIENCE.md` `LiveAirflowMonitorOrganism` #1, `State_MonitoringActive`). *Why it matters:* The task's own suspicion is confirmed — the 0-FPS pure-black screen with a "dim pulsing green heartbeat dot" is a visual-only cue. A blind or low-vision screen-reader user who opens the app mid-session (e.g. to check it's working) lands on a screen with no accessible label, live region, or announced state, and no way to confirm protection is active except by reading the persistent notification from the shade — which requires backgrounding the app they just opened. *Fix:* Give the night-mode screen (or its root semantics node) a persistent accessible label such as "Sleep monitoring active — D-BAND connected" even though it renders nothing visually, and specify it as a bullet in the Accessibility Floor.
- **critical** No persistent "you are NOT protected" signal is specified for the case where BLE permission is denied and the AD-12 foreground service therefore never starts. (`EXPERIENCE.md` `BlePermissionPrimerOrganism` #7, `State_BlePermissionDenied` #11, Do's/Don'ts). *Why it matters:* The spec relies on *absence* of the "Sleep Monitoring Ready" notification to imply "not monitoring." Absence-of-notification is not an accessible or even a noticeable cue — for a groggy or visually-impaired patient this is the worst possible failure mode: believing you're covered when you aren't, with the app spine itself never having designed the negative-confirmation state. *Fix:* Specify a positive, persistent, accessible "Not Protected — Bluetooth permission needed" state (banner and/or notification) that appears whenever the receiver service is not running, not just a one-time blocked screen at `MOB_DEVICE_PAIRING`.
- **high** iOS's one-shot permission-dialog behavior is unaddressed. Once a user denies the CoreBluetooth prompt, iOS will not show the system dialog again on a second tap of the same in-app CTA — the app must detect the prior denial and route to Settings instead. (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Onboarding "Handoff": *"Tapping the CTA triggers the native OS permission flow directly"*; §Interaction Primitives "OS Permission Hand-off": *"no in-app re-prompt loop, no double-asking"*). *Why it matters:* If the primer re-appears on a later boot for a previously-denied user (permission-state-at-boot check per line 57) and they tap "Allow Bluetooth Access" again, iOS will silently do nothing — no dialog, no error, no change of screen. For an anxious or cognitively-vulnerable patient that is a dead-end with zero feedback. *Fix:* Specify that the primer/organism must check prior denial state on iOS and route straight to the Settings deep-link instead of re-issuing a request that cannot fire.
- **high** No mention of Android's `neverForLocation` manifest flag for `BLUETOOTH_SCAN`. (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Onboarding "Handoff"). *Why it matters:* Without it, Android's own system dialog can fold in "approximate location" language the in-app priming copy never mentions ("find your D-BAND sensor... allow Bluetooth access"). This is exactly the confusion/fear scenario the task flags: a low-tech, health-anxious patient sees a breathing monitor suddenly asking for location and denies out of suspicion — precipitating finding #3 above. *Fix:* Add the manifest flag as a build requirement in the spine (or explicitly note it as an architecture invariant) so the priming copy and the native dialog stay in agreement.
- **high** The iOS `NSBluetoothAlwaysUsageDescription` string — the one piece of native-dialog text actually authorable by this team — is never specified or referenced. (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Voice and Tone only lists the in-app primer copy). *Why it matters:* This string appears inside Apple's own system alert verbatim. If UX doesn't author it, engineering will ship placeholder/boilerplate text, and the entire stated purpose of priming ("frame native OS permission dialogs with in-app context first," `DESIGN.md` Do's) breaks down at the one point where it was actually achievable. *Fix:* Add the Info.plist usage-description string to Voice and Tone alongside the primer copy, written in the same reassuring register.
- **medium** `BlePermissionPrimerCard`'s primary CTA ("Allow Bluetooth Access") has no specified background/text color pairing, and DESIGN.md defines no default primary `ShadButton` token anywhere (only the emergency-specific `ApneaAlertBanner` button colors are given explicitly). (`DESIGN.md` §Components #9). *Why it matters:* Contrast compliance ($\ge4.5{:}1$ per the Accessibility Floor) cannot be verified from the spec as written; an implementer could reach for a shadcn default that doesn't clear AA against `#0F172A`. *Fix:* Define the default primary-button color pair in the token table, not just per-component ad hoc.
- **medium** The "reachable" claim in the new Accessibility Floor bullet assumes linear swipe traversal only. (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Accessibility Floor: *"the full permission rationale is read by assistive tech before the button is reachable"*). *Why it matters:* VoiceOver/TalkBack "explore by touch" lets a user land directly on whatever they physically touch first — a user who touches the lower half of the screen hits the CTA before ever encountering the rationale, and nothing in the spec sets initial accessibility focus (e.g. auto-focus to the headline on screen appear) to counter that. Widget-tree order alone doesn't guarantee reading order for a touch-exploring user. *Fix:* Specify programmatic initial accessibility focus on the headline/rationale when the screen appears, in addition to tree order.
- **medium** `MetricStatCard`'s status pill is described only by color ("`#10B981` Green for Normal, `#EF4444` Red for Severe") with no explicit confirmation it carries a text label, unlike `SessionHistoryListItem`'s badge which is explicitly given as `"AHI 3.2 Normal"`. (`DESIGN.md` §Components #1 vs #4). *Why it matters:* As literally worded this reads as a color-only status indicator — a WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) violation if built to spec. *Fix:* Make the text label explicit in `MetricStatCard`'s description, matching the pattern already used for `SessionHistoryListItem`.
- **medium** Emergency alert flashing has no specified flash rate or duty cycle. (`EXPERIENCE.md` `ApneaAlertBannerOrganism` #3: *"pulsates"*; §Accessibility Floor: *"high-contrast flashing red overlays"*). *Why it matters:* WCAG 2.3.1 caps general flashing content at 3 flashes/second; an uncontrolled implementation risks a photosensitive-seizure trigger in exactly the population most likely to have comorbidities. Pre-existing, not part of this pass, but real and unaddressed. *Fix:* Specify a flash rate (or a slow pulse/fade rather than a hard flash) and document the rationale if a safety-alert exemption is being claimed.
- **medium** Possible ambiguity between the notification Do/Don't and its tap behavior. (`DESIGN.md` Do's/Don'ts: *"no re-lighting the screen mid-session"*; `EXPERIENCE.md` `BleReceiverForegroundNotification` #8: *"Tapping opens the app to its current screen"*). *Why it matters:* Read literally and out of context, an implementer could over-apply the "no re-lighting" constraint and suppress the (correct, desired) tap-to-open behavior, since opening the app from a locked/off-screen state necessarily lights the screen. The intent is almost certainly "no *notification-initiated* wake (no heads-up, no sound)," but the two bullets sit far apart and aren't cross-referenced. *Fix:* Add a one-clause cross-reference clarifying the DON'T is about notification-initiated screen changes, not user-initiated taps.
- **low** The Bluetooth glyph icon on `BlePermissionPrimerCard` is never explicitly marked decorative/excluded from the accessibility tree, unlike the rigor the rest of the doc applies to icon labeling. (`DESIGN.md` §Components #9). *Why it matters:* An unlabeled or auto-labeled ("bluetooth icon") node ahead of the headline adds noise to the exact reading-order guarantee the new Accessibility Floor bullet is trying to make. *Fix:* Explicitly mark it decorative in the component spec.
- **low** No alternate confirmation path exists for the Tier-1 "I'M SAFE" dismiss for a patient with a motor impairment severe enough that a single tap isn't reliable (beyond the existing 5s auto-silence-on-recovered-breathing fallback). (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Interaction Primitives "Single-Tap Emergency Dismiss"). *Why it matters:* Pre-existing, not touched by this pass, but this is a sleep-apnea population with plausible comorbid mobility/motor issues, and the only non-tap path (auto-silence) requires breathing to have already recovered — it isn't a general motor-accessibility alternative. *Fix:* Worth a design note even if out of scope for this iteration.
- **low / observation** The Key Flow narrative places the permission-priming screen at bedtime on the first night (10:20 PM), i.e. exactly when the target patient is most likely to be groggy, tired, or eager to just get to sleep. (`EXPERIENCE.md` §Key Flows step 2). *Why it matters:* Not a spec defect, but it works against the product's own "minimize friction/cognitive load at bedtime" ethos to place a permission negotiation (with a native dialog whose wording the app doesn't control) at that moment rather than during earlier daytime setup. *Fix:* Consider whether first-run onboarding (including BLE priming) can be nudged toward initial app install/setup rather than the first bedtime session, if there's any flexibility in the flow.

## Clean areas

The Accessibility Floor's touch-target math checks out (the primer CTA and Bluetooth glyph icon both clear WCAG contrast comfortably against `#0F172A` — verified by hand: `#10B981`-on-`#0F172A` ≈ 7:1, `#94A3B8`-on-`#0F172A` ≈ 7:1). The inert-vs-navigable `SettingsMenuRow` announcement handling (dimmed-but-announced, not silently unfocusable) is genuinely well specified and a good pattern to reuse elsewhere. The "single-path, no skip" framing of the permission primer is a defensible, honest choice given monitoring truly can't function without the permission, rather than dishonestly offering a "maybe later" that leads nowhere. The multi-sensory (visual+audio+haptic) design of the Tier-1 alarm is solid baseline coverage for hearing- or vision-impaired users acting alone (just not both at once, per the motor-accessibility note above).
